# Layerline catalogue and production plan

## Starting point

The Astro shop and Worker share 17 catalogue products. Keep gifts, desk, garage and business products alongside premium décor. The cottage lake terrain map already exists. Extend that listing for framed standard landscapes and a custom location proof. Retain Stripe checkout, invoice-based quotes, private R2 artwork, Access-protected admin and separate test orders.

## Collection and capacity

Use Maison SIA's sculptural silhouettes and the Sheyn Bloz as aesthetic references. Develop original geometry and photograph actual samples. Start with a matte taupe fold vase (230 mm tall, retail test CAD 175), then a chalk decorative tide bowl (210 mm wide, CAD 145), a low companion vase (170 mm tall, CAD 95; pair CAD 245) and a graphite heron (CAD 125). Prioritize the vase and bowl. Test a glass insert for fresh stems. Style the pair together for a shop-the-room purchase. Keep lighting for later fixture development. Test framed terrain at CAD 199, with larger lodge commissions individually quoted.

Assumed printer: Bambu Lab P2S, nominal 256 × 256 × 256 mm. The 280–300 mm inspiration piece exceeds upright build height. Target 140 × 120 × 230 mm for the first vase and 210 × 155 × 70 mm for the bowl. Slice the actual model with the installed nozzle and plate, including brim, support and toolhead clearances. Quote oversized work after testing joints or an alternative orientation. Record elapsed hours, grams, rejects, insert fit, stability and packed shipping weight. Hold only consumables and samples. Set dispatch estimates from available machine hours and materials. Prices and dimensions are prototype targets.

## Delivery sequence

1. Catalogue and printing. Add prototype entries behind the existing sample gate. Give staff a searchable catalogue and individual print sheets from the paid snapshot. Mark test work clearly.
2. Production tracking. Start with paid-order stages. Extend to durable per-line jobs, file revision, quantity, due date, printer, QC, retry history and partial completion. Reserve capacity after confirmed payment. Reprinting one part must preserve the order and charge.
3. Custom work. Connect submitted brief, artwork, quote revisions, customer proof approval and paid invoice to the same production queue. Keep standard finishes direct-purchase and unusual dimensions or volume orders quoted.
4. Fulfilment. Add printable packing slips, pickup, partial shipments, carrier references and a private customer tracking page. Never expose R2 keys or customer details in public status responses.
5. Catalogue administration. Draft and version prices, variants, images, materials, inserts, print profiles, lead times and release approvals. Publish one authoritative catalogue to storefront and server pricing. Preserve purchased specifications in each paid snapshot.
6. Materials and reporting. Track filament by finish, inserts, packaging, printer time, failed batches, actual costs and margin. Extend existing monthly reporting.
7. Release test. Place a test order for one vase and bowl, print the work sheet, reprint only the bowl, pack together and confirm tracking. Test pickup, expired art, webhook retries, concurrent edits, partial shipment and old orders. Sample approval, tax readiness and studio Stripe activation remain launch dependencies.
